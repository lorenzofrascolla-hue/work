Paper-trading research system: demo

A working copy of the research dashboard I built and run myself. You can click through all five screens, look up any of the 25 companies on my research list, and read what every panel does.

What this is and is not. This is a demo, not the live system. It is built from data the system saved on 12 October 2026 and nothing on it updates or connects to anything. Every panel here exists on the real dashboard. Panels that need live market, news or filing feeds are shown with their description but are not computed in this snapshot. The account is a practice account at a broker: every balance, position and order is simulated.

About the data

The demo is drawn from these saved files and nothing else. Each one opens or downloads directly.

  • replica.json: the account, positions, company research run, gate results, factor run, backtest and the desk's run log, as exported from the system.
  • prices.json: one year of official daily closing prices for the 25 companies and the S&P 500 fund, from Nasdaq.com.
  • positions.csv: the 14 positions at the snapshot.
  • equity-history.csv: the account value for every trading day, next to the S&P 500 close.
  • paper-trades-dashboard.csv: every simulated order sent through the dashboard (227 records).
  • paper-trades-automated.csv: the simulated orders planned by the automated morning run (92 records).
  • blocked-orders.csv: the 96 simulated buys cancelled by the exposure limit.
  • stopped-runs.csv: the mornings the automated run stopped itself, with the reason as recorded.
  • risk-gates.json: the 13 gates as rules.
  • summary.json: the headline numbers in one file.
How the numbers were checked

The S&P 500 line never uses the system's own benchmark numbers. It uses official closing values from S&P Dow Jones Indices via the Federal Reserve Bank of St. Louis (FRED series SP500), starting from the close of 2 July 2026, the last close before the account reset. The system's recorded benchmark prices track those closes within 0.3% over the whole period.

The account line is the value the system recorded for each trading day. Rebuilding the latest account value from the official closing prices of the 14 holdings lands within 0.3% of what the system recorded, and the system's two separate records of account value agree within 0.3% on 41 of the 42 days they share. On 22 days the history file held a row from a built-in sample portfolio instead of the practice account; those rows were left out. Four trading days have no usable record and the line runs straight across them.

Company fundamentals, forensic scores, price targets and forecast odds are from the system's own research run on each company's filings, saved on 12 October 2026, shown as recorded. The trend, momentum and volatility gates in the demo are recomputed from the official closes above.

Glossary

Paper trading
Practising with simulated orders and a made-up balance at a broker. No real money moves.
Practice (paper) account
The simulated account that receives the paper-trades.
Account value (equity)
What the account would be worth if everything in it were sold at the latest prices.
S&P 500
An index that tracks 500 large US companies, the usual yardstick for the US stock market. SPY is a fund that tracks it.
Benchmark
The yardstick a portfolio is compared with. Here it is the S&P 500.
Research list (SPG universe)
The 25 companies my research paper points at: grid equipment, power producers, data-center landlords and chip suppliers.
Risk gate
A yes-or-no rule a company must pass before the system will flag it as a buy candidate. There are 13.
Exposure limit
A cap on the total size of the portfolio compared with the account value. The last check before any simulated order is sent.
Circuit breaker
A rule that stops the automated run from adding risk after the account falls too far behind the index, until the rules re-arm it.
Risk rung
How much of its full active exposure the system allows itself at the moment, on a ladder from 20% to 100%.
Moving average
The average closing price over the last N trading days. Price above its average is read as an uptrend.
Momentum
Whether the price is higher than it was a set number of days ago.
Volatility
How much a price swings. Annualised volatility is the size of a typical year's swings.
VaR 95 (value at risk)
The loss a bad day could bring, the kind of day that comes about once in twenty.
CVaR
The average loss on the days that are worse than the VaR day.
Beta
How much the book moves for a 1% move in the S&P 500. A beta of 1.5 means it tends to move 1.5%.
Correlation
How closely two things move together, from 1.0 (in step) to 0 (unrelated).
Effective bets
How many truly separate positions a portfolio holds once size and overlap are counted.
Drawdown
The fall from the highest value reached to a later low.
Active share
How different a portfolio is from its reference mix, from 0% (identical) to 100%.
Altman Z-score
A 1968 formula using five accounting ratios to flag companies at risk of financial distress. Below 1.81 is the distress zone.
Beneish M-score
A 1999 formula that looks for patterns in the accounts that often appear when earnings are manipulated. Above −1.78 is a warning.
P/E, ROE, D/E
Price-to-earnings, return on equity, and debt-to-equity: three standard ratios.
Price target
The system's estimate of what a share is worth, from cash flows and multiples.
Factor model
Scoring companies on a few measurable traits (momentum, value, volatility, trend) and ranking them on the blend.
Z-score
How far a value sits from the average, in units of the spread. Zero is average.
Backtest
Replaying a strategy over past prices to see how it would have done. A simulation, not a record.
CAGR
Compound annual growth rate: the steady yearly rate that would produce the same total change.
Sharpe ratio
Return above a safe rate, divided by volatility. Higher means more return per unit of risk.
Basket (equal weight)
Holding every eligible company in the same amount instead of favouring some.
Veto
A failed quality check (such as the forensic scores) that forces the system to sell a name.
Regime
The market's current weather: trending or not, calm or volatile.
